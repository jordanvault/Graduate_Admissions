My preparation for analytics is not a single course or credential but a decade of quantitative decisions under real constraints.

**Academic foundation.** At the University of Wisconsin–Madison I completed statistics (STAT 301, A; GEN BUS 304, A) and calculus (MATH 221, A; MATH 222, B), graduating with a 3.457 GPA, and studied as an exchange student at the National University of Singapore.

**Professional evidence.** For eleven years at Korean Air I worked in revenue management, operating forecasting and pricing systems. During COVID, I used Python to build logistic regression and random forest models that segmented route-level recovery patterns, which informed demand forecasting and pricing decisions. I also built a SQL-based reservation-misuse monitoring tool that flagged more than 600 suspicious cases a year and prevented an estimated $370,000 in annual revenue leakage.

My largest data project was integrating more than 100 million ticketing and flown records with Delta Air Lines' passenger data within nine months. Reconciling different definitions of customers, bookings, and routes taught me that quantitative work begins with agreeing on what is being measured.

That lesson shaped my recent work at Samsung SDS. When regional teams found that historical logistics volumes changed each time they queried the system, I used SQL to compare monthly house-bill snapshots. More than half of the apparent decline came from re-dated shipments; I classified the rest into four error types. I then led a six-person task force that redesigned four aggregation rules, reduced anomalous records from 0.80% to 0.56% within two months, and recovered 28,306 FEU of misclassified 2025 volume.

**The gap I want to close.** I have learned analytics largely by operating systems others designed. Foster's MSBA would give me the formal grounding in predictive modeling, experimentation, and machine learning to build such models myself and to judge when their outputs deserve trust.
