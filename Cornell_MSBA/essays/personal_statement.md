# Personal Statement (300–500 words) — Draft v3

For most of my career, I was the person colleagues came to for answers. It took leading people in places I did not understand to see how limited that role was.

In 2022, I began leading a team of seven in Manila, Cebu, and Guam while travel was recovering from COVID-19. Our headquarters in Seoul and the local agencies kept reading the same market differently, and I assumed the agencies were being resistant. Then I visited them. The agents knew which customers were returning, which groups were forming, and why, and none of it appeared in the reports I had been reading from Seoul. I rewrote our reporting so their knowledge could reach the people making decisions. Group reservations rose 340% that year, but what stayed with me was simpler: the people closest to a problem usually understand it best, and my job was to make sure they were heard.

Two years later, I left aviation for Samsung SDS and had to learn the same lesson again from the other side. I was suddenly the newcomer, asking questions that would once have embarrassed me. When a freight reporting dispute spread across ten organizations, those questions mattered. Each department's rules made sense for its own operations, and I could not tell which were wrong until I understood what each was meant to measure. Listening first is what allowed a six-person task force to agree on shared definitions that those regions still use.

The same pattern appeared at home. As a parent, I wanted to solve a small daily problem, keeping phones out of my children's sight, and ended up designing and selling a product, AiMollaeBook. I knew nothing about manufacturing or fulfillment, so I asked people who did. Every answer I received made the next decision easier.

I now want to pursue a graduate degree because I have reached the limit of what I can learn on the job. I can ask good questions and make careful use of data, but I need rigorous training in forecasting and optimization, and peers who will challenge how I reason. I have already shown I can study seriously alongside full-time work, earning CPIM and PMP certifications.

At Cornell, I would bring the habits these experiences built: asking the basic question when others hesitate, listening before judging, and making room for classmates whose training or background differs from mine. I have learned that the best work happens in teams where no one is afraid to say what they do not yet understand, and I want to help create that environment.
