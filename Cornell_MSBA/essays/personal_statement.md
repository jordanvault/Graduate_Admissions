# Personal Statement (300–500 words) — Draft v2

My path to graduate study has repeatedly made me a newcomer. I grew up in Korea, studied finance at the University of Wisconsin-Madison, and spent a semester at the National University of Singapore. Each move taught me that competence in one setting does not transfer automatically; I had to learn how people around me reasoned and what they took for granted.

That lesson shaped my career. After years at Korean Air, I led a team of seven across Manila, Cebu, and Guam during the post-COVID recovery. Headquarters and local agencies read the same market differently, and early on I mistook those differences for resistance. Field visits changed my view: the agencies held knowledge that headquarters' reports could not capture. I revised reporting procedures so that knowledge could reach decisions, and group reservations rose 340% year over year. I came to see my role as creating space for the people closest to a problem to be heard.

In 2024, I left aviation for Samsung SDS and became a beginner again. After years of being the person colleagues turned to for answers, I had to ask questions that once felt too basic. When colleagues explained why they measured shipments differently, I learned to withhold judgment until I understood their process. That habit let me lead a six-person task force through a reporting dispute across ten organizations: we first established what each rule was meant to measure before deciding which was wrong, and the shared definitions we agreed on are now how those regions explain their figures.

Becoming a parent added another perspective. Raising two children led me to design and sell AiMollaeBook, a book-shaped product that keeps a phone out of sight. I had no experience in production, marketing, or fulfillment, so I learned each from people who knew more than I did. More than 2,000 units later, I know that I can pick up an unfamiliar discipline quickly and take responsibility for the result.

These experiences are why I am pursuing a graduate degree now, and why I expect to succeed in it. I have kept up serious study alongside full-time work, earning CPIM and PMP certifications, and I know I learn most from people whose backgrounds differ from mine.

At Cornell, I would contribute as someone who has worked across cultures and industries, who listens before judging, and who welcomes questions about my own assumptions. I want classmates to feel free to say what they do not yet understand, because I have benefited from that freedom myself.
