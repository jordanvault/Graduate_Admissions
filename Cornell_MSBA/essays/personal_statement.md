# Personal Statement (300–500 words) — Draft v1

My path to graduate study has involved repeatedly being a newcomer. I grew up in Korea, studied finance at the University of Wisconsin-Madison, and spent a semester at the National University of Singapore. Each move taught me that competence in one setting does not transfer automatically. I had to learn how people around me reasoned, what they took for granted, and what they were reluctant to say aloud.

That lesson shaped my career. After eleven years at Korean Air, I led a team of seven across Manila, Cebu, and Guam during the post-COVID recovery. Headquarters and local agencies read the same market differently, and early on I mistook those differences for resistance. Visiting the field changed my view: the agencies held knowledge that headquarters' reports could not capture. Revised reporting procedures made it easier for that knowledge to reach decisions, and group reservations rose 340% year over year during the recovery period. I came to see my role as creating space for the people closest to a problem to be heard.

In 2024, I left aviation for Samsung SDS and became a beginner again, in a field whose vocabulary I did not yet speak. After years of being the person colleagues turned to for answers, I had to ask questions that once felt too basic. When colleagues explained why they measured shipments differently, I learned to withhold judgment until I understood their process. That habit later helped me lead a six-person task force through a reporting dispute involving ten organizations, because we first established what each rule was meant to measure before deciding which was wrong.

Becoming a parent added another perspective. Raising two children led me to design and sell AiMollaeBook, a book-shaped product that keeps a phone out of sight. More than 2,000 units have sold. It demanded skills I did not have, and I relied on advice from people with far more experience than I had. Doing it while working full time showed me both the cost and the value of pursuing a goal on a demanding schedule.

These experiences are why I am pursuing a graduate degree now, and why I believe I can succeed in it. I have learned to study seriously alongside full-time work, earning CPIM and PMP certifications. I also know that I learn most from people whose backgrounds differ from mine.

At Cornell, I would contribute as someone who has worked across cultures and industries, who listens before judging, and who welcomes questions about his own assumptions. I want classmates to feel free to say what they do not yet understand, because I have benefited from that freedom myself.
