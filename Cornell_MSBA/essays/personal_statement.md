# Personal Statement (300–500 words) — Draft v4

After thirteen years of work, what pushed me toward graduate study was not a lack of experience but the limits of what experience alone could teach me. I could build tools and run analyses, yet I kept reaching questions I could not answer well: why one method suits a problem, how far to trust a forecast, what evidence justifies a decision. I realized that I needed two things I could not get on the job: the theory behind the methods I use, and fresh conversations with peers who do not share my assumptions.

The role that taught me the most about working across differences was the 17-month Group Sales Optimizer project at Korean Air, where I translated sales rules into automated booking workflows. The people involved spoke different professional languages. Sales staff knew pricing and customers but little about systems, IT engineers knew systems but not how group sales works, and external vendors had their own constraints and vocabulary. I became the translator. For sales colleagues, I described requirements in terms of customers and revenue. For engineers, I expressed the same needs as precise logic and data. For vendors, I framed requests in terms of their situation and terminology. No one needed to learn another's field; they needed someone willing to meet them where they were. The project worked because everyone could take part on terms they understood.

I learned the same lesson leading a team of seven in Manila, Cebu, and Guam, where headquarters and local agencies read the market differently. Field visits showed me that local colleagues held knowledge that our reports lacked, and revising our reporting so their voices reached decisions helped group reservations rise 340% year over year. When I joined Samsung SDS in 2024, I became the newcomer, asking questions that once felt too basic. That openness helped a six-person task force settle a reporting dispute across ten organizations by first agreeing on what each rule was meant to measure.

I have also shown I can sustain demanding study. I earned CPIM and PMP certifications while working full time, and, as a parent, I taught myself production and fulfillment to build a product, AiMollaeBook, now past 2,000 units.

At Cornell, I would bring the translator's habits to a cohort of people with varied training, cultures, and strengths: explaining ideas in terms a classmate can use, asking the basic question when others hesitate, and listening before judging. I expect to share what I know about airline and logistics operations and to learn from classmates in finance and marketing. I want every teammate to feel their perspective is needed, because the best decisions come from people who can understand one another.
