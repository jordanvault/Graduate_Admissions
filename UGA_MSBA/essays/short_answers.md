## 1. Career Goals

**Prompt:** What are your short-term career goals immediately after completing the MSBA program? How do those goals connect to your long-term professional aspirations?

**Word count:** 247

Immediately after the MSBA, I want to join a U.S. analytics team working on pricing, capacity, or network decisions in logistics and supply chain, either in-house or in a consulting practice serving those industries. Supply chain analytics or decision science roles would let me apply modeling to problems I already understand from eleven years in airline revenue management and two in logistics analytics.

That goal grows directly from my current work. At Samsung SDS, I led a task force that standardized how six regions and ten organizations counted freight volume, recovering 28,306 FEU of misclassified 2025 volume. Once the number was trustworthy, harder questions followed: which lanes need capacity, where demand is shifting, which customers may leave. I want my next role to answer those questions with predictive and prescriptive models, not only descriptive reporting.

Long term, I aim to lead an analytics function between operations and executive leadership, accountable for data definitions, model design, adoption, and measurable results. My career has shown me that analytics most often fails at the handoff between a model and the people who must act on it. I want to be the leader who owns that handoff.

The CMC is central to this plan. Although I bring thirteen years of experience, nearly all of it is in Korea and Southeast Asia. Coaching on translating that experience for U.S. employers, interview preparation, and connections to Atlanta's logistics, retail, and consulting employers would help me make this transition efficiently rather than start over.

## 2. Technical Experience

**Prompt:** Describe your experience with programming languages or technical tools, such as Python, R, SQL, Tableau, Power BI, Excel, or others. Provide one or two specific examples of how you have used these tools in coursework, projects, internships, or work.

**Word count:** 241

SQL is my primary tool. At Samsung SDS, I query shipment-level logistics data daily. When regional teams reported that historical volumes changed every time they queried the system, I wrote SQL to compare monthly snapshots at the house-bill level. More than half of the apparent decline came from shipments being re-dated from departure to arrival after confirmation; I classified the remaining changes into four error types. That analysis became the basis for four redesigned aggregation rules and reduced anomalous records from 0.80% to 0.56% within two months.

At Korean Air, I built a reservation-misuse monitoring tool that combined SQL queries on booking data with a dashboard for sales teams. It flagged more than 600 suspicious cases annually and prevented an estimated $370,000 in revenue leakage. During COVID, I used Python to build logistic regression and random forest models that segmented route-level recovery patterns, informing demand forecasting and pricing decisions.

I also use Power BI and Oracle BI for dashboards, Google Analytics 4 combined with internal log data to analyze customer journeys on Samsung SDS's Cello Square platform, and Excel for day-to-day modeling. Recently, I have been building a CPIM study web app with Claude Code, which has taught me to review AI-generated code critically rather than accept it.

My gaps are R and formal machine-learning training; my Python has been applied rather than systematic. The MSBA's coursework in R, Python, and machine learning is where I expect to grow the most.
